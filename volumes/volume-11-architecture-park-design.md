# RC WORLD Master Development Plan — Volume 11: Architecture & Park Design

**Volume 11 of 14** · **Revision:** 1.0 — July 2026 · **Status:** Living document — bump revision on material change

**Purpose of this volume.** This volume is the architects' and contractors' brief for the RC WORLD park: the master site plan for the canonical 4.8-hectare reference site, dimensioned zone-by-zone design concepts, building floor plans with room schedules, utilities and infrastructure concepts, and the safety, security and landscape systems that bind them together. It translates the operating doctrine of Volumes 3–7 — sight-line separation, the 15° haul-road limit, the bunkered charging doctrine, the fully netted airfield, the 20-minute Shift throughput model — into buildable geometry: areas in m², heights in mm, gradients in degrees and percent, electrical loads in kVA. It is written so that a design team can take it, a topographic survey, and the local code set, and proceed directly to schematic design. Where this volume states a dimension, every other volume must quote it; where another volume is named as source of truth (fleet counts, pond depth, cage envelope), this volume quotes *it* and says so.

**Intended readers.** The appointed architect, civil/structural engineer, MEP engineer and landscape architect; the main contractor and specialist net/pond/track subcontractors; the founder and General Manager acting as informed client; investors and lenders assessing the physical asset behind the Phase 1 capex (≈ $1.85 M, Volume 10); franchisees adapting the reference design under Volume 12; the permitting consultant preparing planning and building submissions.

**A note on codes.** RC WORLD is planned for a mid-size international city not yet fixed; this volume therefore specifies *performance and geometry*, not statutory compliance. Every item flagged **[verify locally]** — occupancy classifications, fire separations, sanitary fixture counts, accessible-parking ratios, electrical standards, structural wind/seismic loads, pond safety barriers — must be confirmed against the governing local code by the appointed design team before construction documents. Nothing in this volume overrides local law.

## 1. Design Philosophy & Site Selection

### 1.1 The park is the product: industrial-authentic, not theme-park

RC WORLD sells the fantasy of operating a working industrial complex at 1/14 scale. The architecture must therefore *be* industrial, not depict it. The design language is *industrial-authentic*: real materials doing real jobs, detailed honestly, weathering gracefully, photographing like a documentary rather than a stage set. Three consequences follow.

**First, the materials palette is the working world's palette.** Scaffold-tube guardrails, shipping containers as enclosed program, corten and galvanized steel, board-marked and power-floated concrete, timber sleepers, HDPE shade sails in plant colors (safety yellow, machine orange, gunmetal). Nothing is painted to look like something else. A container that houses the marine plant room is labeled MARINE PLANT ROOM in stenciled DIN lettering, because that is what a port operator would do — and the stencil *is* the theming.

| Element | Canonical material & finish | Where used |
|---|---|---|
| Guardrails & operator rails | 42 mm galvanized scaffold tube, key-clamp fittings, rail height 1.1 m (operator) / 1.1–1.2 m (spectator) | All zones, walkways, rostrums |
| Enclosed program (plant, stores, kiosks) | 20 ft / 40 ft high-cube shipping containers, corten patina or RAL 7016/1023 repaint, container-code graphics retained | Marine plant, parts overflow, F&B kiosk, marshal posts |
| Feature cladding & signage plates | 1.5–3 mm corten steel sheet, laser-cut lettering, unfinished | Zone gateways, wayfinding totems, hopper cladding |
| Walking surfaces | Compacted crushed stone (guest paths), power-floated concrete (buildings, pit lane), timber boardwalk (crawler zone, pond quay) | Site-wide |
| Retaining & pit edges | New/reclaimed timber sleepers 200 × 100 mm, galvanized pin fixings | Mining rim, berms, planters |
| Shade | HDPE knitted shade sails, 90%+ UV block, on 76–114 mm galvanized posts | Operator stations, rostrums, queues |
| Roofs (buildings) | Trapezoidal profile steel sheet, 6° min pitch, exposed gutters and downpipes to visible rainwater channels | The Works, entry pavilion, restaurant |

> **Field Note** — The cheapest theming decision in the whole park is *restraint*. Every fake rock, cartoon graphic or plastic-log fence added "for the kids" dilutes the premium that Volume 2's market research identified: adults pay because the place feels like a real site they are finally allowed to run. Kids do not need help pretending; adults do. Build for the adults' disbelief and the kids come free.

**Second, scale discipline.** The park operates two scales simultaneously: 1:1 for humans (rails, counters, doors) and roughly 1/14 for the machine world (dig faces, crop rows, quay bollards). The two must never blur. Human furniture is deliberately plain and industrial so the eye reads it as "backstage"; the machine world is detailed to scale-model fidelity (Volume 2, Chapter 4 sources the dressing; Volume 4 specifies the mining scenography). Rule of thumb for designers: *anything a camera at machine height can see is scenography and must be to scale; anything only a standing human sees is plant and must be honest.*

**Third, the sight-line doctrine: see everything, touch nothing live.** This is the load-bearing safety idea of the entire park, inherited from the source doctrine (customers never walk onto live tracks; Tow-Truck Retrieval Protocol instead) and it is architectural before it is procedural:

- Every operator station gives an unaided, unobstructed view of the operator's machine and its full working envelope — no station may require operating into a visual shadow (this rule fixed the mining hopper's position, Volume 4, Chapter 6).
- Every live surface (track, pit, pond, cage, field) is separated from guests by a physical edge — rail, fence, level change, water — of at least 1.1 m effective height, with kick-plates where objects could roll under. Gates into live areas are staff-controlled and interlocked with zone status in RC WORLD OS.
- Spectating is a designed product, not a leftover: terraces, mounds, the grandstand, and the elevated mining walkway (Chapter 9) are placed so that a non-paying companion always has somewhere good to stand, in shade, with the action framed for a phone camera. Volume 2's finding that "media is the marketing department" is implemented here as geometry.
- Nothing that is live is reachable, and nothing that is reachable is live. Dead vehicles are retrieved by the recovery crawlers (gameplay) or by staff; the architecture removes the temptation by making the reach physically impossible, not merely forbidden.

> **Safety Warning** — The 1.1 m rail is the *minimum effective* barrier height and assumes level standing ground on the guest side. Wherever guests stand on raised terraces, mounds or walkways, the barrier must be re-derived from the local fall/reach condition and checked against the governing building code **[verify locally]**. The sight-line doctrine never justifies lowering a barrier; if a rail blocks a child's view, the ground is raised (step, mound) — the rail is not lowered.

### 1.2 Design tenets for every consultant

1. **Throughput is geometry.** The 20-minute Shift model (canon) means every attraction is sized around simultaneous stations × turnover, not around track length or pond area for their own sake. Queue, briefing, hand-over and retrieval space is program, not circulation.
2. **Maintenance access is a first-class user.** Every zone has a staff-side service edge reachable by the utility vehicles and, for mining/marine/airfield, by a light truck. If an Artisan cannot reach a feature with a toolbag in under two minutes, redesign it.
3. **Phase lines are clean lines.** Phase 2 and 3 works (Chapter 2.4) must be buildable behind construction fencing without closing Phase 1 zones or crossing guest circulation. Utilities are stubbed to phase boundaries at Phase 1 (Chapter 12).
4. **Design for the hose and the broom.** Dust, mud and water are the park's daily weather. Falls to drainage everywhere, no un-drained low points, washdown points at every zone, surfaces that a leaf-blower and a hose can reset in 30 minutes each morning.
5. **Wind is a program input.** The airfield's orientation, the pond's sheltering berms and the shade-sail engineering all begin from the site wind rose (Section 1.4 checklist; Volume 5, Chapter 2 and Volume 6, Chapter 2 state the operating wind limits this plan must protect).

### 1.3 The canonical reference site

All dimensions in this volume are laid out on the **canonical reference site** (style-guide canon): **4.8 hectares (~12 acres)** of leased peri-urban land in a mid-size international city; **flat, with a 2–4% natural grade** falling to the southwest corner; **road frontage** on the southern boundary; **municipal three-phase power and water** at the frontage. The reference plat is taken as a rectangle **240 m (E–W) × 200 m (N–S)** = 48,000 m²; a real site of different proportion re-flows the master plan (Chapter 2) but must preserve the adjacency logic, the noise gradient, and every zone's internal dimensions.

The 2–4% grade is an asset, not a defect: it drains the site to a single southwest detention point (Chapter 11), gives the mining rim its elevation for free at the north end, and is imperceptible to guests. Sites steeper than ~5% average impose retaining costs that break the Phase 1 budget; sites dead flat impose drainage pumping. The band is deliberate.

### 1.4 Site-selection criteria checklist

The following checklist is the go/no-go filter for the reference site and for every franchise site under Volume 12. Items marked ● are hard requirements; ○ are strong preferences that trade against each other.

- [ ] ● **Area:** ≥ 4.5 ha contiguous and developable (4.8 ha reference); regular shape, no dimension < 150 m.
- [ ] ● **Grade:** average natural grade 1–5%; no more than 15% of the site steeper than 8%.
- [ ] ● **Frontage & access:** direct frontage to a public road able to take coaches and articulated delivery vehicles; sight distances at the access point adequate for a leisure use **[verify locally]**.
- [ ] ● **Power:** municipal three-phase supply at or near the frontage, upgradeable to ≥ 200 kVA demand (Chapter 12 load schedule); note the utility's connection lead time in the project program.
- [ ] ● **Water & sewer:** municipal potable water at frontage; sewer connection or approved on-site treatment feasible **[verify locally]**.
- [ ] ● **Planning/zoning:** leisure/recreation use permitted or realistically permittable; no residential facade within 100 m of the northern (operational) half of the plan, or acoustic report demonstrates compliance **[verify locally]**.
- [ ] ● **Flood:** outside the locally defined high-risk flood zone; if in a moderate zone, finished floor levels and the detention strategy (Chapter 11) resolve it **[verify locally]**.
- [ ] ● **Tenure:** lease ≥ 10 years with renewal option, or purchase; landlord consent for the netted airfield's 16–17 m poles obtained in principle before signing.
- [ ] ○ **Catchment:** ≥ 500,000 population within 45 minutes' drive (Volume 2, Chapter 7 demand model).
- [ ] ○ **Wind:** 12-month wind rose available or commissioned; prevailing direction steady enough to orient the airfield within ±20° (Volume 5 canon); mean speeds compatible with marine operating windows (Volume 6).
- [ ] ○ **Soil:** free-draining granular soils preferred (tracks, pond excavation, pole foundations); a Phase 0 geotechnical investigation with ≥ 6 trial pits and 2 boreholes at the airfield and arena positions is a pre-contract requirement.
- [ ] ○ **Aviation constraints:** site outside airport obstacle-limitation surfaces and any UAS restriction zone that would prohibit even netted model flight; confirm the 17 m poles trigger no aviation-obstacle process **[verify locally]**.
- [ ] ○ **Neighbours:** no dust-sensitive or noise-sensitive neighbour (hospital, kennel, precision industry) sharing the northern boundary.
- [ ] ○ **Visibility:** frontage visible to passing traffic (the netted cage and corten gateway are the billboard).
- [ ] ○ **Transit & coach:** public transport stop within 500 m; school-coach approach without reversing.

> **Investor Note** — Volume 2 identified land as a structural barrier to entry: 4–5 contiguous peri-urban hectares with utilities are scarce exactly where demand is strongest. The checklist above is deliberately strict; a site that fails two ● items is a walk-away regardless of price, because every failure lands in Phase 1 capex or in the operating calendar (wind, flood) forever.

### 2.2 Adjacency logic

The plan is an argument, and each adjacency is a clause of it:

- **Mining borders The Works** (precincts 7 and 9 share an edge) because the Construction Division has the park's heaviest assets, the highest per-Shift maintenance event rate (Volume 4), and the only bulk-material logistics: aggregate top-ups, hopper maintenance and the nightly face re-cut all run from The Works' yard through a single staff gate. The mining hopper sits at the zone's east end (fixed in Volume 4 by the sight-line rule) — which is exactly the Works-facing end, so the conveyor and weighbridge electronics are a 20 m walk from the electronics bench.
- **The Works sits on the eastern service spine**, front door facing the guest path (the viewing window, Chapter 6, makes Artisan work a spectacle), back door facing the service road, so parts deliveries and fleet movements never cross a guest route.
- **The Airfield is peripheral and downwind: the northeast corner.** Three independent reasons converge. (1) *Risk geometry* — although the cage makes flyaway physically contained (Volume 5), defense in depth wants maximum distance between flight operations and both the entry crowds and the public road. (2) *Noise* — EDF and prop noise is the park's loudest continuous source; with the prevailing southwesterly of the reference wind rose, the northeast corner exports noise away from the entry, the restaurant and the frontage neighbours. (3) *Structure* — the 16–17 m poles (Volume 5 canon) want the longest possible setback from the boundary the planning authority cares about, which is the road frontage. The cage's long axis runs E–W, within ±20° of the prevailing wind — this volume's siting tolerance on Volume 5's requirement that the runway and circuit work into wind.
- **Marine and Agriculture are the calm band**, west-center, adjacent to each other: the two quietest, most family-skewed zones share the gentlest part of the noise gradient, the events lawn, and the afternoon shade of the western berm. The harbour pond sits in the wind shadow engineered by that berm plus its own shelter belt (Volume 6 makes wind shelter a revenue item), while the sail area's windward shore is deliberately kept low and open (Volume 6, §4.2 and §6.6 doctrine); the speed pond's long axis is set across the prevailing wind to cut fetch, and the restaurant sits between the harbour and the spine so its terrace overlooks the working port (Volume 6's siting requirement) with Track A's straight in the second sight line.
- **Track A fronts the social heart.** The asphalt track is the park's most legible spectacle, so it sits center-east where the pit lane and grandstand overlook it, and the (Phase 2) restaurant's second, east-facing terrace front catches its straight. The drag straight aligns with the grandstand's axis.
- **Track B and Track C occupy the northwest**, between Agriculture and Mining: dirt with dirt. Their dust sources sit downwind of the guest core on the prevailing wind and their service needs share the northern staff route.
- **Entry, parking and the Phase 3 arena hold the south frontage.** Everything a visitor does before paying — parking, drop-off, ticketing — stays south of the guest gateway; the arena (Phase 3) also fronts south so corporate-event traffic never enters the park proper.

### 2.3 The noise gradient

Sound is planned as a south-to-north crescendo. At the frontage: car doors and café murmur. Through the gateway: PA at conversation level, the pond's calm. Mid-park: Track A tire noise and commentary. North band: mining buzzers, Track B's jumps, the airfield's motors — all held against the northern landscape berm (Chapter 11), which is both acoustic screen and viewing mound. Design targets, to be confirmed by the acoustic consultant against local limits **[verify locally]**: ≤ 55 dB(A) LAeq at the southern boundary, ≤ 50 dB(A) at any residential facade. The gradient also works commercially: the entry experience stays unhurried (retail dwell), and anticipation builds as guests walk north into the machine noise.

## 3. Zone Design — Motorsport

The Speed & Off-Road Hub is three engineered surfaces plus the shared driver infrastructure. Volume 3 owns everything about the cars and the racing program; this chapter owns everything you can pour, compact, bolt or plant. Where Volume 3 stated interface requirements (its Sections 9.6, 10.4, 11.4, 13.2), they are restated here as built geometry.

### 3.1 Track A — asphalt/drift circuit

**Layout geometry.** Track A is a closed circuit of **~180 m racing line** within a 60 × 45 m precinct pad, designed bidirectional (Volume 3 canon: league nights may invert direction, so barrier profiles, kerb ramps and rostrum sight lines are symmetrical about the racing line). The canonical layout:

| Parameter | Specification |
|---|---|
| Racing line length | 180 m (–0/+10 m as built; report as-built to Volume 3 for lap-parity math) |
| Lane width | **3.5 m minimum, 4.5 m** on the main straight and outside of Turn 1 (widest where speeds are highest and passing is intended) |
| Main straight | **58 m** between corner tangent points — satisfies the ≥ 55 m drag requirement: 40.2 m strip + ≥ 15 m braking run-off (Volume 3 canon) |
| Corner set | 8 corners: T1 R=6.0 m 90°; T2–T3 chicane R=3.0 m; T4 hairpin R=2.5 m (minimum radius on circuit); T5 R=8.0 m sweeper 120°; T6 R=4.0 m; T7–T8 esses R=3.5 m onto the straight |
| Minimum corner radius | 2.5 m (hairpin) — below 2.0 m, 1/10 touring cars scrub instead of steer |
| Crossfall | 1.5–2.0% uniform to the outside edge (drainage; imperceptible to handling at this scale) |
| Drift annex | 15 × 12 m polished pad joined at T5, own entry gate, sharing the T5 sweep as a linked layout (Volume 3, Section 9.6) |

**Surface build-up (section, top down):** 25–30 mm fine-graded asphalt wearing course (6 mm nominal aggregate, machine-laid — hand-laid asphalt leaves grip inconsistencies the parity program will find) *or* 120 mm power-floated concrete slab with a light mechanical polish on the drift annex; 150 mm compacted crushed-stone base (CBR ≥ 30); compacted subgrade; geotextile separator on soft spots. Joints in the concrete option are sawn, not tooled, and sealed flush — a 3 mm lip is a launch ramp at 1/10 scale. Surface tolerance: 3 mm under a 3 m straightedge anywhere on the racing line.

**Kerbs.** Corner apexes and exit edges carry **scale FIA-profile kerbs**: precast concrete or machined HDPE strips 300 mm wide, rising 15 mm at 1:10 chamfer, painted red/white in 250 mm alternations. Kerbs are glued and pinned, not just bedded — a loose kerb is a projectile. The kerb face never exceeds 15 mm: at 60 km/h a 1/10 car treats 15 mm as a rumble, 25 mm as a wall.

**Run-off and barriers, in order of energy absorbed:** (1) painted run-off apron 0.5–1.0 m wide in the same surface, (2) a 0.3 m crushed-rubber-granule strip at high-energy corners (T1, T4, end of straight), then (3) the **barrier system**: 200 mm diameter foam pool-noodle-over-rope barrier on 12 mm polypropylene rope threaded through galvanized eye stakes at 1.0 m centers — the industry-standard RC track barrier, cheap, continuous, and energy-absorbing with no hard edges. Behind it, at the spectator edge only, a rigid 1.1 m scaffold-tube rail with 150 mm timber kick-plate. The two-stage system means a 60 km/h drag car meets foam first, steel never. Marshal access gaps every 15 m, offset so no straight-line path from track to public exists.

**Embedded services (cast in at construction, per Volume 3):** MYLAPS RC4 timing-loop conduit (25 mm) across start/finish plus a spare loop conduit mid-straight for the drag trap; 50 mm power/data conduit ring for trackside cameras and (Phase 3) floodlighting; drainage channel at the low edge discharging to the swale network (Chapter 11).

**Construction sequence and QA hold points.** Track A is the one surface in the park where the contractor's habits from full-scale roadwork actively mislead: tolerances that pass on a highway fail here. Sequence: (1) subgrade proof-roll, witnessed; (2) base course laid and compacted to ≥ 98% modified Proctor, surveyed on a 5 m grid, ±10 mm; (3) timing and lighting conduits set and *sleeved through, not under*, the base; (4) wearing course machine-laid in maximum two passes with a hot longitudinal joint on the racing line's least-loaded axis — never mid-corner; (5) 72-hour cure/traffic hold; (6) acceptance survey: **3 mm under a 3 m straightedge anywhere on the racing line, no ponding after a hose test, joint lips ≤ 1 mm**. A failed panel is cut back to the nearest joint and re-laid — surface patching is prohibited on the racing line because the parity program (Volume 3, lap times within 2–3%) will detect a grip patch faster than any straightedge. The drift annex's mechanical polish is a separate visit after cure, with a skid-value target set jointly with the Motorsport Lead from test-car sessions: polish is sneaking up on a number, not executing a spec.

### 3.2 Track B — dirt rally circuit

**Layout geometry.** ~**160 m racing line** in a 55 × 42 m precinct, packed-dirt surface, 4.0 m nominal width (4.5 m on the jump straight), designed unidirectional (jumps have a direction; inverting Track B is forbidden — the landing ramps only work one way).

**Surface engineering.** 100 mm of screened clay-bound gravel ("road base with fines", 10–15% clay fraction) laid over 100 mm compacted crushed stone, watered and rolled to a hard, slightly plastic running crust. The clay fraction is the maintenance variable: too little and the surface ravels to marbles, too much and rain closes the track. The track crew's daily water-and-roll reset (Volume 3, Section 10.4 moisture doctrine: "dark but not shiny") is enabled by a dedicated hose ring with take-offs every 20 m and a walk-behind roller garaged at the zone.

**Jump profiles and the safe-landing ratio.** All jumps are **tabletops** (never doubles — a cased double destroys rental fleet at industrial rates; a cased tabletop is just a flat landing) built to Volume 3's canon of **≤ 0.5 m height, landing-matched**:

| Element | Dimension | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| Take-off ramp | Rise 0.5 m over 1.5 m run (18°) with the last 300 mm relaxed to 12° | The relaxed lip stops rental buggies from over-rotating; a sharp lip is a backflip machine |
| Table (deck) | 2.5 m level | Cars landing short land flat |
| Landing ramp | Fall 0.5 m over **2.5 m** run (11°) | **Landing length ≥ 5 × jump height** — the safe-landing ratio this volume fixes. At the 50 km/h rental cap, flight is 3–5 m; an 11° landing matches the descent angle so suspension absorbs mm, not the chassis |
| Transition radii | ≥ 1.0 m at ramp toe and crest | No kinks: a kink is a load spike |
| Construction | Compacted fill core faced with 75 mm clay-gravel wearing layer over geogrid; timber-sleeper edge boards flush with surface | Jumps that shrink under traffic become doubles by erosion — the geogrid and edge boards hold profile |

Two tabletops sit on the jump straight, one single "kicker" tabletop at 0.3 m height on the back section. A whoops section (five rollers, 150 mm high at 1.2 m pitch) provides the technical suspension test without airtime.

**Berm construction.** Bermed corners are the dirt track's kerbs: compacted fill banks on the outside of corners T2, T5 and T7, **inner radius per corner (2.5–5 m), bank face rising to 400 mm at 30–35°**, faced with the same clay-gravel wearing course over a geogrid-wrapped core, sleeper-edged at the back. Berms are cut with a 100 mm flat shelf at the top so an overshooting car parks rather than launches. Rebuild profile is checked monthly against painted depth gauges set in the berm core.

**Drainage.** Dirt tracks die by water. The whole precinct is crowned at 2% to perimeter french drains (100 mm perforated pipe in 300 × 300 mm gravel trench, geotextile-wrapped) discharging to the northern swale; jump and berm cores are free-draining fill so they don't slump; and the marshal posts stand on 100 mm raised concrete pads (Volume 3 requires them splash-safe). After the design storm the track must be rollable within 2 hours — that criterion, not a pipe size, is the spec the civil engineer must meet **[verify locally]** for local rainfall intensity.

### 7.3 Electrical load — worked example

Circuit sizing for the charging room, as a worked example the MEP engineer scales for the Works bunker (this method, with local code factors, is the deliverable — the numbers are the reference case) **[verify locally]**:

1. **Pack energy.** Reference pack: 2S 7.4 V nominal × 5 Ah = **37 Wh** (3S 11.1 V × 5 Ah = 55.5 Wh). Fleet mix ≈ 70/30 2S/3S → weighted ≈ 42.6 Wh/pack.
2. **Charge power per channel.** Doctrine charge rate 1C (5 A) at the 4.2 V/cell termination voltage with charger losses (η ≈ 0.85): 2S channel ≈ (8.4 V × 5 A)/0.85 ≈ **49 W**; 3S ≈ (12.6 × 5)/0.85 ≈ **74 W**. Weighted ≈ 57 W/channel.
3. **Connected load.** 10 chargers × 4 channels × 57 W ≈ **2.3 kW** charging + charger overhead/fans ≈ 0.3 kW + room services (lighting, extract fan, monitoring) ≈ 0.8 kW → **≈ 3.4 kW connected**.
4. **Demand.** All channels genuinely run together at peak turnover (diversity 1.0 on charge circuits — never diversify a battery room), room services diversity 0.8 → **design demand ≈ 3.2 kW ≈ 3.5 kVA at PF 0.95**. Note the packs arrive ~70% depleted at most (the canonical ~30% buffer returns with every Casual Shift), so the 1C session runs ≈ 50 minutes including the CV tail — the number step 6 uses.
5. **Circuits.** Two 20 A radial circuits per rack (5 chargers/rack, ≤ 1.5 kW/circuit — 50% loaded, cool and expandable), one 16 A services circuit, all 30 mA RCD-protected; sub-board 3-phase 32 A/phase with 40% spare ways. The Works bunker, with ~2.5× the channel count for the 450-pack fleet rotation, scales to ≈ 9 kVA by the same method.
6. **Throughput check.** 40 channels × ~50 min/pack ⇒ ≈ 48 packs/hour through the customer station — comfortably ahead of peak swap demand (8 counters × ~2 swaps/counter/Shift-block ≈ 48 packs/hour at full house), which is the point: the electrical design and the Shift model are the same calculation.

Three rules govern species selection (local ecologist adapts the list **[verify locally]**): drought-tolerant once established (irrigation is for turf and hedges only), no thorn species within reach of guest paths, and — critically — **low-FOD near the airfield**: within 30 m of the cage, no species that sheds cones, large seed pods, catkins or twiggy debris that ends up on the runway or in the nets. Evergreen, small-leaf, deep-rooting species (holm-oak/olive/pittosporum types in temperate-dry climates) around the cage; deciduous shade trees are welcome at the plaza, restaurant and southern spine where their leaf-fall lands on sweepable paving; ornamental grasses and gravel-garden planting dress the berms (industrial-authentic: the palette of a rehabilitated quarry, not a resort).

### 11.3 Dust suppression

Dust is the operating cost of a dirt park: Track B's watering ring (§3.2), mining's nightly wet-grooming (Volume 4), the northern service road dressed in crusher fines with a calcium-chloride/lignin binder refreshed each season, and hose points at 40 m spacing on the irrigation main. Wind-blown dust from the loose zones is intercepted by the berm-and-hedge line before it reaches the ponds (skimmer load and viewing-bay clarity) or the airfield (runway grip). The morning "hose-and-broom reset" (design tenet 4, §1.2) is a designed capability: every zone has a washdown point, falls, and a silt trap.

### 11.4 Stormwater and drainage strategy

The 2–4% SW grade organizes the whole strategy: a network of grass swales along the path edges collects zone runoff (each zone discharging through its own silt trap — mining's fines and Track B's clay never reach the network raw) and feeds the **SW detention basin** (~600 m³, side slopes 1:4, max design depth 600 mm, dry between storms, planted as a rain garden) which throttles discharge to the municipal system or watercourse at the permitted rate **[verify locally]**. Hard-surface ratio at build-out ≈ 31% (Chapter 2.1); car-park outer bands in permeable grid pavers reduce the peak. The pond is hydraulically independent (lined, top-up metered); its overflow weir discharges to the swale network. Design storm and discharge consent are jurisdictional **[verify locally]** — the layout reserves the basin footprint for the wettest plausible case.

### 11.5 Shade strategy

Shade is a throughput input: dwell collapses on unshaded summer sites. Layered provision — engineered sails over every operator station, rostrum and queue (§§3–7); canopy trees at plaza, lawn and spine seating; built canopies at grandstand and spectator quay; and the buildings' own deep eaves on southern facades. Target: at 14:00 on the summer design day, **≥ 60% of all designed guest positions (stations, seats, queues) are in shade**. Sails come down for the storm season per the maintenance calendar (wind rating on every sail post is a structural item **[verify locally]**).

### 13.4 Emergency preparedness

- **First-aid points:** the pavilion first-aid room (§8.1, ambulance-accessible) plus wall stations (AED, trauma kit, burn kit) at the pit theatre, The Works and — Phase 2 — the marina. Burn kits acknowledge the LiPo risk profile.
- **Assembly points:** two — the events lawn (north assembly) and the car-park head (south assembly), signed from every zone; choice of two means no route past a plausible incident (fire at The Works blocks neither).
- **PA:** zone horns on the AP/CCTV poles, addressable by zone from the duty office and the OS duty console; the all-park evacuation tone is generator-backed.
- **Lightning protection:** the airfield's 17 m poles are the site's air terminals whether planned or not — so plan it: the cage poles are bonded and earthed as a lightning protection system by the electrical designer **[verify locally]**, the flight program's storm rules (Volume 5) close the cage well before risk, and the same weather trigger closes both ponds (Volume 6's 30/30 water-clear protocol) and clears the mining rim's sail line. A site lightning-risk assessment is a Phase 2 design deliverable.
- **Fire zones and extinguisher schedule:** the site is zoned to the detection panel as: Z1 pavilion/retail, Z2 The Works, **Z2B bunker**, Z3 pit/charging, Z4 restaurant (Ph. 2), Z5 arena (Ph. 3), plus external plant nodes. Extinguisher family **[verify locally — types/spacing are code items]**: ABC dry powder at all plant and vehicles; CO₂ at electrical boards and comms; wet chemical at kitchens; **Class D capability + sand quench bins at both charging rooms and the battery room**; travel distance to an extinguisher ≤ 20 m back-of-house.

> **Safety Warning** — The interlock chain (OS zone status → gate release → marshal badge) is only as safe as its failure mode. Specify every live-zone gate **fail-secure for entry, fail-safe for exit**: a network or power failure must never trap a person inside a zone, and must never release public entry into one. This requirement is written on the door-hardware schedule, tested at commissioning, and re-tested quarterly (Volume 7 maintenance matrix).
