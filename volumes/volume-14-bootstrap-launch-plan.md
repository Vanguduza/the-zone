## 1. Why Start Small

### 1.1 The bootstrap thesis

The master plan's core commercial claim — that simulation-of-work is a durable, differentiated entertainment format (Volume 4, Chapter 1) — has been argued from market research, pilot testing and analogy. It has not yet been proven with a stranger's money crossing a counter. The bootstrap thesis is that this claim can be proven for $10,000, because the park's single most differentiated product happens to be its cheapest to stage: **the Construction/Mining experience works indoors, in 300 m², with machines that cost $130–500 each, on a medium (sand) that costs almost nothing and never wears out.** Racing needs a track, grip surfaces and 20–30 standardized cars before the product exists (Volume 3). Aviation needs a netted cage (Volume 5). Marine needs ponds (Volume 6). Construction needs a pit, a loop and eleven machines — and unlike every one of those formats, it has effectively no competitor anywhere (Volume 2's competitive-landscape finding: public-access RC earthmoving is close to nonexistent worldwide). The cheapest experiment available to this business is also the one aimed at its emptiest market. That coincidence is the whole reason this volume exists.

### 1.2 What the Micro-Park is and is not

The **RC WORLD Micro-Park** is a single-zone, founder-operated dig-and-haul floor: two excavators loading six dump trucks on a one-way haul loop, a wheel loader and a dozer for variety and grooming, eight operator stations, a paper roster and a phone timer. It sells the same billing unit as the master plan — the **20-minute Shift** — under the same operating doctrine: 1:3 excavator-to-truck ratio, 3:1 battery pool in the 3.4–4.2 V/cell window, XT60 standard, customers never enter the dig zone. It is a real RC WORLD in every rule that matters.

It is *not* a smaller copy of Phase 1, and pretending otherwise is how $10,000 becomes $40,000. Deferred explicitly, per the bootstrap canon (style guide, "Bootstrap edition canon"):

- **Hydraulics — deferred entirely.** No Kabolite-class machine anywhere in the bootstrap fleet. The master plan's own two-tier doctrine (Volume 4 §1.3, §8.7) shows why: a hydraulic machine on public duty carries 5–7× the maintenance cost per Shift, multi-day failure modes, oil, seals and pumps — precisely the purchase and upkeep burden a $10K launch cannot carry. The first hydraulic unit is **Rung 2 of the upgrade ladder** (Chapter 9), bought from operating proceeds as a supervised premium experience, exactly as the doctrine prescribes.
- **RCW Node telemetry and RC WORLD OS** — replaced by a phone timer, a spreadsheet and the founder's eyes (Chapter 5). Restored at Rung 4.
- **All other divisions, all buildings, all staff** beyond an optional weekend helper.

### 1.3 The two documents rule

From launch day the business runs on two documents with two different jobs. **Volumes 1–13** are the destination: shown to landlords who ask "what is this, really?", to partners, and eventually to investors — unchanged, unhedged, canonical. **Volume 14** is the vehicle: the budget actually spent, the checklist actually walked each morning, the ladder actually climbed. When the two conflict on a number, Volume 14 governs Year 0 operations and Volumes 1–13 govern everything the money has not reached yet. The discipline this buys is real: the founder never has to argue the $2.6 M plan down to $10K credibility or inflate the $10K reality up to investor scale — each document does its own job.

> **Investor Note.** For the eventual Rung 5 reader: nothing in this volume revises the master plan's economics. The Micro-Park is a demand experiment and an operator apprenticeship, deliberately run at the bottom of the pricing ladder in a bootstrap market. Its unit economics (Chapter 6) are not the Phase 1 model's (Volume 10, Chapter 4) and are not meant to be; what transfers is the demand signal, the retention data, and a founder who has personally rebuilt every gearbox in the fleet.

### 1.4 What "proof" means

The experiment has defined success criteria, set now so they cannot be moved later: **(a)** sustained paid utilization — 300+ Shifts/month by Month 6 (Chapter 6's base case); **(b)** repeat behaviour — ≥25% of weekend customers returning within 90 days (the micro-scale echo of Volume 10's second-visit KPI, tracked by punch card); **(c)** a birthday-party pipeline — ≥3 bookings/month by Month 6; **(d)** cash — monthly net inside the Chapter 6 corridor. Hit all four and the upgrade ladder is live. Miss them past the Chapter 10 stop-loss criteria and the honest conclusion is recorded, the fleet is sold at the Chapter 10 recovery rates, and the master plan waits for a better catchment — having cost $7,000-odd to test instead of $2.6 M.

## 2. The $10,000 Budget

### 2.1 Budget rules

Three rules governed the construction of this budget. **First, honest small-quantity pricing.** At 2–8 units the founder does not get Volume 8's container-wholesale rates. The digest's canonical wholesale bands (Huina 1580 at $350–420, 1582 at $150–200) assume trade-volume orders; a bootstrap buyer on Alibaba/AliExpress at 1–5 pieces pays ex-works prices only ~10–20% below those bands **and then pays freight on 7–11 kg boxed machines**, which erases most of the gap versus a good Western dealer sale. Every fleet line below is a *landed* estimate built from verified July 2026 quotes at bootstrap quantities (sources and ranges in Chapter 4). **Second, the site is a cost, not an afterthought.** The budget carries a lease deposit and first month — the line most $10K plans omit and then die on. **Third, contingency is sacred.** ~10% sits untouched at the bottom; Section 2.3 says what it is for and Section 2.4 says what to cut instead of spending it.

### 2.3 What the contingency is for

The $950 exists for exactly three events, in priority order: **(1) freight and customs shocks** — small-shipment import costs are the least predictable line in the budget and can run $150–300 over estimate on a bad consolidation; **(2) a dead-on-arrival machine** that the seller disputes — assume one of eleven, budget its replacement; **(3) landlord surprises** — a demanded extra bond, a required electrical inspection, a floor-loading certificate. It is not for a nicer banner, a twelfth machine, or "while we're at it" theming. Whatever remains at opening day becomes the operating cash float (Chapter 6.5 assumes $300+ float at open).

## 3. The 300 m² Micro-Park

### 3.1 Design doctrine, inherited and scaled

The Micro-Park layout is Volume 11's Construction Zone doctrine passed through a 1:8 area reduction (2,400 m² → 300 m²) with every safety-relevant dimension preserved and every theatrical dimension sacrificed. Preserved: the **operator rail at 1.1 m** with a step for children (Volume 4 §10.3), the **hard customer/machine separation** — customers operate from the rail, full stop (Volume 4 §10.4) —, haul grades under the canonical **15° ceiling**, the one-way haul loop, the charging area physically segregated behind a barrier, and unobstructed sight lines from every station to its machine and its haul segment. Sacrificed: the two-bench pit (one bench at micro scale), the load-cell hopper (a grizzly box on a $25 luggage scale), the premium pad, the terrace, all shade structures (indoors) and all landscaping.

Key dimensions and features:

- **Entry & pay desk (2 × 3 m, NE corner):** one table, card reader, waiver clipboard, punch-card box, price board. The only place customers transact.
- **Spectator/queue strip (1.5 m deep, along the north wall):** behind the operator line. Parents watch from here; the queue self-organizes along it. At micro scale spectating *is* the marketing — the strip faces the whole floor.
- **Operator rail:** 8 stations at ~2 m centres, rail at 1.1 m, a 250 mm timber step stored under stations 1–3 for small children (the under-8-with-guardian pairing, Volume 4 §10.3). Transmitter lanyard hooks on every station — the single cheapest breakage-prevention device the master plan ever tested.
- **Dig pit A (6 × 5 m):** timber-edged, floor at −300 mm (one bench; sand depth 300 mm over the protected floor), two dig faces cut to the Volume 4 §6.2 template at 30–40°, one marked truck spot per face.
- **Haul loop (~30 m lap):** one-way, counter-clockwise, 900 mm formed width in compacted sand over membrane, single ramp out of the pit at ≤14° (rising 0.3 m over ≥1.2 m of run — build it at 1.5 m and it is 11°, comfortably inside the 15° canon).
- **Hopper/dump point (SE):** a 500 × 500 mm timber grizzly box with 20 mm bar spacing sitting on a 50 kg luggage scale; the founder reads and calls out each delivered load. It is the tonnage leaderboard with the electronics removed — and the ritual of the call-out ("Four point two kilos! New leader!") replaces the screen.
- **Dozer box (3 × 3 m, SW):** fenced from the loop; the 1554 lives here for the youngest operators, exactly per Volume 4 §10.1's dozer-box logic.
- **Charging & maintenance corner (4 × 3 m, NW, behind a barrier):** charging table with the Bat-Safe box on a paver tile, battery crates (IN / READY / STORAGE), the tool roll, the spares tub, and the bench that is also the repair bench. Customers never pass the barrier. This corner is The Works, the battery room and the charging bunker of Volume 7 compressed to twelve square metres — the fire-discipline rules compress with it (Chapter 7.5).
- **Service lane (1 m, south edge):** staff-only walking route to the hopper and pit without crossing customer space.

### 3.3 Indoor bay vs outdoor yard

| Factor | Indoor warehouse bay | Outdoor fenced yard |
|---|---|---|
| Weather | Immune — every booked Shift happens; winter revenue survives | Rain and heat kill days; seasonal revenue swing of 30–50% |
| Dust | The dominant problem: fines accumulate on every surface. Controls: washed <3% fines sand (C2 spec), 4–6% moisture kept up with a hand mister, membrane under everything, no dry sweeping — mist first (Volume 4 §6.6 scaled) | Self-managing; wind replaces the mister as the enemy (dry faces blow) |
| Floor | Must protect slab: poly membrane + sacrificial OSB at edges (C5); landlord will ask (checklist below) | Ground is the floor; grading and drainage needed; mud risk |
| Security | Roller door + landlord alarm; fleet sleeps on site | Theft/vandal exposure; fleet must be carried home nightly or a lockbox built (~$200, not budgeted) |
| Light & power | Existing circuits likely; work lights fill gaps | Power may not exist at all — a genuine disqualifier for charging discipline |
| Hours | Evening sessions possible year-round → weekday after-school revenue (Chapter 6) | Daylight-bound unless lighting is added |
| Ambience | Echoey; a $30 speaker and machine sounds fix most of it | Naturally atmospheric; the golden-hour haul shot (Volume 4 §10.4) is free marketing |
| Rent | Higher per m² | Lower, sometimes near-free from a farm market or FEC partner (Chapter 8.4) |

**Recommendation:** indoor bay, unless the local market offers an outdoor partnership with existing footfall (a farm market, a family entertainment center's yard). Weather immunity protects the one asset a solo operator cannot replace — bookable, predictable hours — and the charging corner's fire discipline is far easier to guarantee under a roof the founder controls.

### 3.4 Utilities

- **Power — required: 2–3 ordinary circuits.** Circuit 1: charging corner only (two B6neos at full load draw under 500 W combined — trivial, but the circuit is dedicated so a trip never kills the till). Circuit 2: lights, speaker, card reader, phone chargers. Circuit 3 (nice-to-have): future expansion / kettle. Every charging-corner connection through an RCD/GFCI adapter (C9).
- **Water — nice-to-have, not required.** The mister consumes ~10 L/day, carried in jerry cans if no tap exists. A tap on site saves labour and enables end-of-day hand-washing; it moves a candidate site up the list but disqualifies nothing.
- **Toilet access** — required in practice for parties (90+ minute dwell). A shared facility in the building or within 50 m is acceptable; none at all is a soft disqualifier for the party channel that anchors Chapter 6 revenue.
- **Internet** — phone hotspot suffices for the card reader and WhatsApp. Do not pay for a line in Year 0.

### 3.5 The landlord conversation

Walk in with the one-page concept sheet (a photo of a Huina 1580 digging, the layout sketch, the insurance quote) and walk the answers to this list before any handshake:

- [ ] Is **sand on the floor** acceptable with membrane protection, and is a make-good clause (sweep-out + membrane removal) sufficient at exit?
- [ ] What is the **floor loading** and slab condition? (Our load is trivial — ~15 t of sand spread over 40 m² is ~0.4 t/m² — but ask before they do.)
- [ ] Is **public access** permitted under the building's use class / zoning? Children on premises?
- [ ] Confirm **operating hours** allowed — evenings and weekends are the business.
- [ ] Any objection to **LiPo battery charging** on site? (Show the Bat-Safe box and the Chapter 7.5 regime — leading with the answer beats defending the question.)
- [ ] **Power**: how many circuits in the bay, breaker rating, RCD present? Meter — who pays?
- [ ] **Water/toilet** access, and shared-facility arrangements for visitor use?
- [ ] **Insurance**: does the landlord require being named on the policy? Minimum cover figure?
- [ ] **Lease terms**: month-to-month or 6-month minimum? Deposit amount? Break clause? (Target: 6 months with a 2-month break — long enough to test, short enough to stop-loss.)
- [ ] **Signage** rights at the road/door, and can a banner go up for launch weekend?
- [ ] Any **noise** constraints? (Machines are quiet; excited children are not.)
- [ ] Who else uses the building on weekends — shared access, forklift traffic, door schedules?

> **Trade Hack.** Offer the landlord a family session voucher with the first rent payment. A landlord whose grandchildren have driven the excavators renews leases, forgives sand in the corridor, and becomes the first word-of-mouth node in the building. Cost: $16 of Shift value. Return: a relationship the stop-loss clause hopes never to test.

## 4. The Bootstrap Fleet

### 4.1 The doctrine, restated for one buyer

The fleet is **metal, electric, electromechanical — lead-screw actuation throughout — and contains no hydraulic machine.** This is not a compromise forced by the budget; it is the master plan's own rental doctrine (Volume 4 §1.3) applied at the scale where it matters most. A lead-screw machine has no fluid to leak, no pump to score, no seals to cook; its failure modes are a $1 micro-switch, a $6 gear set, a $10 servo — kitchen-table fixes from a shelf tub (Chapter 7). Its lead screws are self-locking, so a loaded bucket held mid-air draws zero current. Its spares are the cheapest and most available in the entire RC construction market. For a founder who is simultaneously the operator, the Artisan, the marketer and the accountant, *artisan-repairable* is the single most valuable property a machine can have. Hydraulic machines (Kabolite class) are deferred to Rung 2 of the ladder — bought from proceeds, run supervised at premium pricing, exactly as Volume 4 Chapter 8 prescribes.

The fleet honours the canonical **1:3 excavator-to-dump-truck ratio** (derived in Volume 4 §5.2) at micro scale: two front-line excavators, six trucks, plus a loader, a backup excavator and a dozer. All prices are verified July 2026 market observations at bootstrap quantities.

**Sourcing honesty.** The landed estimates assume a mixed strategy: trucks and small machines ordered as one consolidated small-lot direct shipment (observed Alibaba tiers: 1582 at $115/1 pc, $105/2–9 pcs, $99/≥10 pcs; 1580 at $295–299/1–5 pcs), with freight, duty and clearance realistically adding 30–60% at these weights (a boxed 1580 ships at ~11 kg). If consolidation falls through, the retail fallback works: a 1580 V5 at $499 from a discount dealer costs the same as a bad direct import. **Do not** chase the digest's $350–420 wholesale band for the 1580 at quantity two — that band is real (Volume 8 documents the channel) but it belongs to trade-volume orders. At Rung 4 quantities it returns.

**Why these seats.** The 1580 is the canonical rental excavator (Volume 4 §2.1) — genuinely all-metal, 23 channels, ~40 min runtime, the machine the entire experience is designed around; two units run two pods. The 1583 adds the V-cycle loading game and doubles as the grooming machine (Volume 4 §4.3). The 1592, at $155–180, is the honest budget insurance policy: a semi-metal machine that covers a 1580's downtime, serves as the junior excavator seat, and costs less than one weekend of lost excavator revenue. The 1554 gives six-year-olds a machine of their own in a fenced box and back-blades the loop at close (the 1569 it descends from is out of production as of 2026).

**Considered and rejected.** *Huina 1573* ($145–165): $100+ saved per truck versus the 1582 buys a smaller working payload and an ABS-dominant build; at six trucks total, fleet uniformity (one spares set, one battery bay, one repair pattern) is worth more than $360 — the 1573 enters at Rung 1 only if truck prices spike. *Huina 1593* ($275–330): a fine machine occupying the dead zone between the 1592's price and the 1580's presence. *Double E E010* (licensed Volvo EC160E, all-metal, $650–980): a genuinely excellent excavator that costs 1.3–2× a 1580 for the same seat — a hobbyist's choice, not a fleet operator's. *Generic/rebadged "full-metal" marques* (Tongde-style listings on AliExpress, 20–30% under Huina money): no parts ecosystem, no version continuity, no community knowledge base — disqualifying for a rental fleet whose economics live in the spares tub. *LESU/JDModel/Kabolite anything*: hydraulic, premium, Rung 2+ (Volume 4 §2.3).

### 4.3 What NOT to buy yet

No premium crawlers (TRX-4 class), no racing fleet, no aviation, no marine, no FPV, no 3D printer, no compressor, no second transmitter case "just in case." Every one of these has a volume of the master plan waiting for it and none of them earns a dollar in a 300 m² dig park. The bootstrap fleet's job is to be **one product, deep** — the mistake that kills micro-parks is being three products, shallow. The budget's most dangerous week is the one after the fleet arrives, when $950 of contingency sits next to an open browser tab; Section 2.3 exists for that week.

## 5. Solo Operations

### 5.1 The operating model

One founder-operator runs the whole floor; an optional part-time helper works weekend peaks (worth hiring from the first month weekend fill exceeds ~50% — Chapter 6 costs this). The design constraint on every procedure in this chapter is **one pair of hands and one pair of eyes**: anything requiring two simultaneous locations is redesigned or deleted. The master plan's staffing (Volume 10, Chapter 5) waits at Rung 3.

### 5.2 Session format — the wave

Free-flow arrivals are unmanageable solo. The Micro-Park runs **waves**: every customer in a wave starts and ends together on one phone timer.

1. **Seating (minutes 0–5):** the founder seats up to 8 operators at stations, issues transmitters from lanyard hooks, delivers the Toolbox Talk briefing (§5.6) to the whole line at once, stages machines.
2. **The Shift (minutes 5–25):** one 20-minute timer. The founder walks the rail — coaching, calling hopper weights, watching for trouble. The canonical Shift is preserved exactly: same billing unit, same ~30% battery buffer returned (billing decoupled from battery life, per the founder's original doctrine).
3. **Reset (minutes 25–30):** transmitters back on hooks, machines re-staged, battery decisions made (§5.4), next wave seated.

One wave = 30 minutes → **2 waves/hour**. A **Double Shift** (the Operator Shift at micro scale) books two consecutive waves with a battery-swap "pit stop" between — the swap performed by the founder at the rail, 90 seconds, theatre included.

### 5.3 Capacity math

| Parameter | Value | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| Operator stations | 8 (2 excavator, 5 truck, 1 loader/dozer rotating) | §3.2 layout; 6 trucks support 5 truck seats + 1 rotating spare |
| Shifts per hour, theoretical | 16 (8 seats × 2 waves) | |
| Shifts per hour, solo-realistic | 10–12 | Reset friction, mixed-age seating, one payment queue |
| Weekend selling day | 6 hours → 96 Shift-slots | e.g. 10:00–13:00, 14:00–17:00 with a grooming/lunch gap |
| Weekday after-school block | 3 hours → 48 slots | Indoor variant only |
| Realistic weekend sold | 20 (slow) / 40 (base) / 60 (strong) | 21% / 42% / 63% slot fill — Chapter 6 uses these |

The excavator seats are the demanded ones (they always are — Volume 4 §5.2's "most expensive boredom" logic in reverse): the roster alternates customers through them across waves, and the truck seats' hopper-scoreboard game keeps them genuinely fun rather than consolation prizes. At a ~30 m lap the truck cycle shortens to ~5.5 minutes (load 3.0 + away 2.5), so the 1:3 ratio actually runs truck-rich at micro scale — the slack absorbs child-pace driving and the third truck doubles as the hot spare during battery swaps. The ratio is kept at 1:3 anyway: it is canon, it is correct at every future scale, and surplus truck capacity at a kids' venue is a feature.

### 5.4 Battery rotation SOP — one person

**SOP MP-001 — Micro-Park Battery Rotation**
**SOP ID:** MP-001 · **Rev:** 1.0 · **Owner:** Founder · **PPE:** none beyond shop standard · **Tools:** cell checker, marker, three labeled crates (IN / CHARGING / READY) · **Frequency:** every reset + continuous background

1. Consumption arithmetic, known in advance: excavators draw 25–40% per Shift → swap every second wave; trucks drive continuously → swap every second wave without exception; loader/dozer as trucks (Volume 4 §9.4 cadence, unchanged).
2. At each reset, pull packs flagged by the roster's simple rule: **any machine completing its second Shift since last swap gets a fresh pack.** No voltmeter debates mid-rush; the rule is conservative by design.
3. Pulled pack → cell-check (10 s). Any cell < 3.5 V: mark the machine's next inspection "over-run — investigate" (a customer or a tired pack; find out at close). Any cell < 3.4 V: pack goes straight to the STORAGE crate for evening attention — the 3.4 V floor is absolute canon.
4. Pulled packs → IN crate. Between waves, load the two B6neos from IN, oldest first, 1C, inside the Bat-Safe box. Chargers run *only* while the founder is in the building — no exceptions, ever (§7.5).
5. Charged packs rest ≥ 20 minutes, then → READY crate. Never issue a pack still warm from charge.
6. Throughput check: two chargers × ~40 min/pack ≈ 3 packs/hour regenerated vs. ~4–5 consumed at full floor — the READY crate therefore starts each day **full** (this is why the pool is 3:1, and why the open checklist's first line is "count READY").

### 5.5 Daily checklists

**Opening (45 minutes before first wave):**

- [ ] READY crate counted: ≥ 18 charged, rested packs (full floor day) or ≥ 12 (weekday); shortfall → start chargers now
- [ ] Walk the floor: barriers and rail secure, step stools staged, no foreign objects in pit or loop
- [ ] Mist sand faces to 4–6% moisture — the face must hold a bucket-cut without slumping (Volume 4 §6.6 opening test)
- [ ] Re-cut both dig faces to template; loose toe heap present; loop swept of yesterday's spill drifts
- [ ] Function-test all fielded machines: 60 s each — tracks, slew, boom/stick/bucket to both limits, listening for switch click; tip test on each truck
- [ ] Transmitters on hooks, matched to machines, all set switches taped (config drift rule, Volume 4 §2.2)
- [ ] Hopper box seated on scale, scale zeroed with box weight
- [ ] Fire extinguishers in place, pins intact; first-aid kit stocked; charging corner barrier closed
- [ ] Card reader on and test-tapped; cash float counted; waiver clipboard loaded; roster sheet dated
- [ ] Phone charged ≥ 80%; timer app open; music/speaker on
- [ ] Price board and open sign out; one launch-quality photo of the clean floor for today's story post

**Closing (30–40 minutes):**

- [ ] All packs to storage charge (3.80–3.85 V/cell), counted into STORAGE crates inside ammo cans / LiPo bags; chargers unplugged at the wall
- [ ] All machines function-tested *while dirty* (faults announce themselves at close, not at open); defects tagged with tape + note for the evening bench
- [ ] Machines brushed down — dry brush, never compressed air indoors, never water (Volume 4 §12.3)
- [ ] Faces re-cut, loop back-bladed (1554), spill reclaimed to source areas
- [ ] Hopper box emptied to stockpile corner; scale stored dry
- [ ] Roster totals → spreadsheet: Shifts sold, revenue, packs cycled, defects, incidents, best hopper weight (the daily record feeds every Chapter 6 decision)
- [ ] Cash reconciled against roster; float locked

- [ ] Lights, locks, alarm

### 5.6 Booking, timing and the Toolbox Talk at micro scale

**Booking** runs on WhatsApp Business + Instagram DMs against a paper/spreadsheet roster: sessions listed as wave times ("Saturday 10:00, 10:30, 11:00…"), each wave a row with eight seat cells. Deposits are not taken in Year 0 — no-shows are absorbed by walk-ins, and the friction of payment-before-visit costs more bookings than it saves. The founder confirms each booking with a fixed message template that carries the three rules (below), the price, and the location pin. **RC WORLD OS is deferred, not disdained** — the roster's columns (wave, seats, machine, packs cycled, defects) are deliberately the future `queue_roster` and `fleet_inventory` fields (Volume 13), so Rung 4's data migration is a typing exercise.

**Shift timing** is one phone timer for the wave — visible, loud, and announced at T-5 ("last hauls — get your load to the hopper!"), which recreates the app's T-5 top-up moment as a human sentence. If seats are free in the next wave, the founder offers the extension on the spot: the Top-Up Session, spoken.

**The Toolbox Talk** compresses from digital induction (Volume 9) to a 90-second verbal briefing delivered to the seated wave, plus a **printed waiver** signed once per customer (kept alphabetically in a binder; the reverse side is the punch card's registration form). The briefing script, verbatim:

1. "Drive from the rail. Nobody crosses it — if a machine gets stuck or dies, that's my job, and watching the rescue is the best part." (The Tow-Truck Retrieval Protocol survives at micro scale as founder-performed theatre: the 1583 or a spare truck nudges and tows casualties out while the wave watches.)
2. "Smooth hands win. These are real machines with real gearboxes — slow is smooth, smooth fills buckets. Slamming the sticks just empties your battery."
3. "The hopper is the scoreboard. Every kilo you deliver gets weighed and called. Today's record is on the board."

> **Field Note.** The wave format's hidden gift is the *shared clock*: eight strangers finishing together produces spontaneous applause at the final hopper call in a way that staggered sessions never do. Protect it — resist the temptation to slot latecomers into a running wave. They wait five minutes, watch, and want it more.

## 6. Pricing & Revenue at Micro Scale

### 6.1 Anchoring: a local-market decision

The master plan's canonical Casual Shift is **$15** (Volume 10 §4.4; style guide pricing anchors). The Micro-Park deliberately prices below that anchor, for stated reasons rather than timidity: it offers no theming depth, no F&B, no license ladder, no telemetry scoreboard, and it launches in whatever market the founder can afford — typically one where $15 for twenty minutes is a hard family sell. **The Shift price is therefore a local decision made once, from a ladder, and held for six months** (repricing monthly destroys the punch-card math and the word-of-mouth quote). The ladder:

| Tier | Casual Shift | Double Shift (2 blocks + pit stop) | Market fit |
|---|---|---|---|
| **$5** | $5 | $9 | Low-income market, secondary city, or aggressive penetration launch |
| **$8** | $8 | $14 | Reference case — mid-income city, indoor bay, this chapter's arithmetic |
| **$12** | $12 | $21 | High-income metro, strong footfall partner venue; nearest to master-plan anchor |

Rule of thumb for choosing: price the Shift at roughly **the local price of a cinema child ticket ±25%**. Parents benchmark against exactly that spend.

### 6.2 Packages

- **Birthday party (the anchor product):** 10 children × 2 Shifts each + exclusive floor for 90 minutes + host duties (the founder runs it as two long waves) + winner's certificate at the hopper. Price at ~1.6× raw Shift value: **$120 / $180 / $260** by tier. One party = 20 Shifts of revenue in a pre-booked, weather-proof, midday-Saturday block, plus eight families who have now seen the park.
- **School/group visit (weekday daytime):** 20–25 children rotating three activities (dig, haul, dozer box) across 90 minutes with a "how machines work" talk — genuinely curricular for ages 6–12. **$150 / $220 / $320**. Sold to teachers as zero-prep excursions (Chapter 8.4).
- **Punch card:** 10 punches → 1 free Shift (paper, stamped, named — the Gears loyalty system's paper ancestor, §8.5). Effective ~9% discount for the heaviest users only.

### 6.5 Cash discipline

Open with the contingency remainder (≥ $300) as float. Each month's net splits by fixed rule: **50% to the upgrade fund** (the ladder's fuel), **30% founder's draw**, **20% to the reserve** until the reserve holds three months of fixed costs (~$3,000), after which its share joins the ladder. The rule is written here so that in Month 4, when the upgrade fund could *almost* buy the hydraulic excavator early, the volume itself says no (Rung 2 has a trigger, and "almost" is not it).

### 6.6 When the numbers say "expand"

Expansion is triggered by the dashboard, never by enthusiasm (Volume 4 §9.5's law, inherited whole). The specific gates live on the ladder's rungs (Chapter 9); the general signal is **three consecutive months at base-case-or-better utilization with weekend waves ≥ 70% full and a party pipeline ≥ 3/month.** One great month is weather; three is a market.

> **Investor Note.** The Micro-Park's blended revenue per machine-day at base case (~$13) is a different animal from the master plan's ~$25 effective block yield (Volume 10 §4.4) — lower price tier, no premium classes, no attach revenue. The number that *does* transfer to the prospectus is utilization behaviour: if a bare-concrete 300 m² bay sustains 40+ Shifts per weekend day at $8, the demand curve behind Volume 2's catchment model has been observed in the wild, at the price floor, with zero brand.

### 7.2 The daily inspection routine

Maintenance without a workshop is **inspection with consequences**. The routine is the PM matrix of Volume 4 §12.2 collapsed to what one person sustains:

- **Daily (in the open/close checklists):** 60-second function test per machine to both limits; connector glance; listen — a healthy lead screw hums, a dry one squeals, a worn nut knocks (Volume 7's bench-test ear, trained in week one).
- **Weekly (Monday evening, 90 minutes, the whole fleet):** track tension by finger-press (3–5 mm sag on 1580/1583, per SOP-CD-001, Volume 4 §12.5); steering linkage shake-test on all trucks; pivot slop check at bucket teeth (<2 mm); every micro-switch clicked by hand.
- **Monthly (one long evening):** lead-screw wipe-and-read + rice-grain re-grease per SOP-CD-005 (glitter on the wipe = order the nut now); tip-limit switches; one gearbox opened per week on rotation so the whole fleet gets a quarterly look without a quarterly marathon.

### 7.3 The top-5 failure points on Huina 15xx machines — and their fixes

Ranked from the Volume 4 §12.7 FMEA, re-weighted by micro-park observation conditions (no telemetry to catch what eyes miss):

| # | Failure | Symptom | Fix | Time / cost |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Limit micro-switch fails** (welded closed or fail-open) | Function runs past its stop and stalls, or won't move at all; motor audible | Diagnose by continuity *before* soldering (half of "dead switches" are misadjusted stops); replace KW11-class switch, set trip 1–2 mm before the hard stop — that margin is the switch's entire job (SOP-CD-002) | 25 min / $1 |
| 2 | **Truck steering servo dies** | Truck won't steer or chatters at center | Swap servo, verify saver gives before servo does | 20 min / $10 |
| 3 | **Nylon gear strips** (shock load — the berm jump, the dropped bucket) | Motor spins, function dead | Open only the failed gearbox, swap gear set, sparing re-grease; check tooth roots on neighbours under the loupe while open | 20–30 min / $6–10 |
| 4 | **Wiring chafe / connector failure at the 1582 bed pivot** | Phantom "dead battery," intermittent power on tip | Re-route loom clear of the pinch line, heat-shrink, pull-test every plug; the refurb electrical pass exists for this loom specifically | 15 min / $2 |
| 5 | **Thrown track / walked track pins** | Machine immobile, track hanging | Re-tension to sag spec (never drum-tight — over-tension eats bushings and raises drive current 15–20%); re-stake proud pins, bin any pin that walks twice | 10–15 min / $0–2 |

Honourable mention: **lead-screw nut wear** (boom droop under load) — slower to develop, announced by the monthly wipe-read, and the reason two nut pairs sit in the spares tub.

### 7.4 Retire or repair?

A machine earns **repair** while the fix is a discrete part from the tub and the cumulative parts spend sits under 40% of replacement cost. It earns **retirement to the parts shelf** when: the chassis/casting itself cracks (Huina castings are not economically weldable), repairs recur at the same organ three times in a season (the FMEA says the machine, not the part, is the problem), or a newer fleet standard makes its spares orphan stock. A retired 1582 is not a loss — it is $60 of servo, motor, ESC, wheels and bed hardware on the shelf, which is precisely the "ghost machine" provisioning of Volume 4 §12.4 achieved by other means.

### 7.5 Battery care absolutes

The master plan puts charging in a cinderblock bunker (Volume 7, Chapters 10–11). The Micro-Park cannot, so the rules compress and *harden*:

- **The 3.4 V/cell floor is absolute.** No pack below it is reissued without an evening bench check; a pack that hit the floor twice is retired from front-line rotation.
- **Charge at 1C, in the Bat-Safe box, on the paver tile, only while present.** The building being "just next door" is not present. Two chargers maximum — pool arithmetic (§5.4) was sized so that this rule never feels like a constraint worth breaking.
- **Storage charge 3.80–3.85 V/cell every night.** Full packs sitting full is how packs die young; the READY crate is charged *ahead of* need each morning, not stored full overnight, except the morning's first six.
- **Physical storage: steel ammo cans and LiPo bags, charging corner, nothing flammable within 1 m.** The $80 of bags and cans (B6) plus the $130 box (B5) is the bunker, scaled.
- **A puffed, dented or hot pack retires immediately** to an ammo can outdoors, discharged, and disposed via municipal battery collection. Never re-tested "one more time," never binned in general waste.
- **Fire response order, posted on the corner barrier:** people out → dry-powder extinguisher on *surroundings* (a LiPo in runaway cannot be extinguished, only contained) → sand bucket on the pack → doors open → do not re-enter for equipment.

### 7.6 Spares reorder discipline

The tub carries the §4.5 kit at par. The reorder rule is **order at half-par, order monthly, ride economy shipping** — at China-direct lead times of 2–6 weeks for small parcels, the half-par trigger keeps the shelf ahead of the fleet without express-freight panic buys. Every part fitted is a tally mark on the tub lid; the tally *is* the failure database until RC WORLD OS arrives (Rung 4), and three tallies against one machine's same organ triggers the §7.4 retirement question.

> **Trade Hack.** Photograph every gearbox before first disassembly and keep the album in a phone folder named per machine. The 1580 uses four visually similar screw lengths, and a long screw driven into a shallow boss cracks the casting — the most common self-inflicted injury to these machines (Volume 4 §12.6). At the kitchen table, the photo album is the shop manual.

## 8. Marketing on a Shoestring

### 8.1 The asset: machines digging is proven viral material

Scale construction machinery working real material is one of the most reliably viral content categories on short-form video — the master plan's marketing strategy (Volume 9) builds on this, and the Micro-Park gets it for free. The launch playbook assumes **zero paid reach until organic proves the hooks**, then spends E6's $500 amplifying only what already works.

**Content system (30 minutes/day, phone + tripod, E8 kit):** one 15–40 s clip per operating day. Post natively to Instagram Reels, TikTok and YouTube Shorts; every caption ends with the booking line and the WhatsApp number. Local hashtags + geotag always — the catchment is a 20–30 minute drive-time, and the algorithm needs telling.

- **Local Facebook groups / community boards:** where parents actually plan weekends in most bootstrap markets. One good clip per week, posted as a person, not a brand.

### 8.3 Free demo events — seeding demand

Before and just after opening, take two machines and a 2 × 2 m sand tray (a folding pool liner in a timber frame — $40, contingency-exempt because it reuses fleet sand) to where families already are: **mall atriums, school fairs, farm markets, toy shops.** Offer 3-minute free drives against a signup to the WhatsApp list. One good mall Saturday reliably yields 100+ list signups and the launch weekend's first sold-out waves. Cost per event: fuel and a table — the E6 budget's cheapest conversions.

### 8.4 Partnerships

The Micro-Park's smallness is a sales feature to venues with footfall and dead space: **cafés and family entertainment centers** (cross-promotion or even hosting — an FEC yard is a candidate site, §3.3), **farm markets** (weekend demo residency), **schools** (the §6.2 group product, sold to teachers as a zero-prep excursion with a genuine mechanisms-and-forces angle), **toy/hobby shops** (flyer swap; they sell the toy, the park sells the experience — non-competing and mutually legitimizing). Every partnership is asked for one thing only: presence in front of their audience. Revenue-share complexity waits for Rung 3.

### 8.5 The birthday channel and the regulars

**Birthdays are the anchor revenue** (§6.2): pre-booked, midday, weather-proof, and self-marketing — every party seats eight future customers' families in the spectator strip. The booking ask goes into every party-adjacent conversation, and party photos (permissioned) are the best-converting content format after the satisfying cycle.

**Regulars become "Site Foremen":** the punch card's completion (10 stamps) confers the title, a hi-vis sticker badge, first call on new-machine debut waves, and their name on the records board. It is the Gears loyalty system and the license ladder (Volume 9) reduced to paper and a marker — and it preserves the same psychological core, *visible standing earned through machine hours*, so the eventual app migration (Rung 4) inherits a community that already understands progression, not just discounts.

> **Field Note.** Answer every single comment and DM for the first six months, personally, fast. At micro scale the founder's responsiveness *is* the brand's customer experience outside the building — and the DM asking "do you do parties?" is the highest-intent lead the business will ever receive at zero acquisition cost.

## 9. The Upgrade Ladder

### 9.1 How the ladder works

Every rung has a **trigger** (evidence, not enthusiasm — cash saved in the upgrade fund per §6.5, plus a demand signal), a **cost**, and an **expected effect**. Rungs are climbed in order; skipping a rung means borrowing against evidence that does not exist yet. The ladder is the operational meaning of the bootstrap canon's growth path: Micro-Park → expanded micro fleet → hydraulic premium tier → multi-zone mini-park → master plan Phase 1.

### 9.2 Rung 1 — Deepen the core (fleet + battery expansion)

- **Trigger:** weekend waves ≥ 70% full for 6 consecutive weeks, *or* recurring walk-aways logged on the roster; upgrade fund ≥ $700.
- **Cost: ~$600.** Two more Huina 1582 (landed ~$330), 12 more value packs (~$220), spares top-up ($50).
- **Effect:** 8 → 10 concurrent seats (truck seats 5 → 7); peak capacity +25%; battery pool stays 3:1 by construction (pack pool grows with the machines, same purchase order, no exceptions — Volume 4 §9.5's rule, inherited verbatim). Expected +$400–700/month at unchanged fill. Note the ratio: with 8 trucks against 2 excavators the fleet runs 1:4 — deliberately truck-rich, because the marginal micro-park customer is a child who wants a truck seat *now*, and the excavator queue is managed by wave rotation. The canonical 1:3 is restored at Rung 3 when the third 1580 arrives.

### 9.3 Rung 2 — The first hydraulic machine (premium tier opens)

- **Trigger:** three consecutive months of net ≥ $1,500; upgrade fund ≥ $3,500 *after* the §6.5 reserve is intact; and the demand signal that matters — regulars and party parents asking "is there a bigger one?"
- **Cost: ~$2,000–2,200 all-in.** An entry-class hydraulic excavator: **Kabolite K961-100S, $1,700–1,900 verified 2026 street** (the K961 is the successor of the K336GC that Volume 4 §2.3 designates as the second-premium/trainer class; hydraulic circuit, brushless pump, FlySky ST8 radio, ripper/breaker/claw attachments) — or **Kabolite K963 crawler loader at $1,185–1,750** as the contrarian pick (a hydraulic machine class nobody has seen, even on video). LESU compact hydraulics (~$2,000+) are the second source (Volume 8). Add ISO VG 32 oil, a seal kit on the shelf, a leak tile and a lockable dust cover: ~$150.
- **Operating format:** the **Master Operator session**, exactly per Volume 4 doctrine — supervised one-on-one, founder present throughout, never in the walk-up rotation, **priced at 2–3× the Shift** ($16–24 at the $8 tier), booked ahead. The machine works its own clean corner of the pit, parks over its leak tile, and gets the rod wipe-down and level glance every single session (Volume 4 §8.6's schedule, no exemptions).
- **The commitment, stated plainly:** this purchase imports the master plan's entire hydraulic maintenance chapter (Volume 4, Chapter 8) into a one-person operation — fluid changes and bleeding (SOP-CD-003), seal kits at 150–300 run-hours, contamination paranoia in a building whose business is sand. It is affordable *only* because it is one machine, supervised, low-hours. A second hydraulic unit before Rung 4 is a doctrine violation.
- **Effect:** 6–10 premium sessions per weekend → +$250–500/month; more importantly, the aspiration engine — the K961 does for the Micro-Park what the K970 does for Phase 1 (Volume 4 §2.3): it makes the whole floor legible as a ladder worth climbing, and its content (real hydraulics, real oil, gauge close-ups) out-performs every other clip format.

### 9.4 Rung 3 — Second zone + first staff

- **Trigger:** net ≥ $2,000/month for 3 consecutive months; birthday bookings ≥ 4/month; upgrade fund ≥ $2,500.
- **Cost: ~$1,800–2,500.** Option A — **crawler corner**: a 2 × 8 m technical strip (timber, rock, bridge) + 6 × MN99S/WPL C24-class crawlers ($45–60 each, digest canon) + packs ≈ $900; Option B — **agriculture strip**: 6 × Double E E351-class tractors + starter implements ≈ $900 (Volume 4, Chapter 7's format at 1/10 scale of itself). Plus the third 1580 + two more trucks (~$820) restoring 1:3 across three pods, and formalizing the weekend helper (~$350/month recurring).
- **Effect:** dwell time and repeat-visit variety — the second zone is bought for *retention*, not capacity; capacity came at Rung 1. Party formats gain a rotation station; the helper unlocks the founder's first full weekend off in a year (Chapter 10 takes burnout seriously; so does this rung).

### 9.5 Rung 4 — The 1,000–1,500 m² mini-park

- **Trigger:** 12 consecutive profitable months; savings + reserve ≥ $12,000; weekends effectively sold out for a quarter; a lease candidate at 3–5× current area.
- **Cost: $15,000–30,000** (from savings, retained earnings, and — if needed — a micro-loan against the trading history, not equity). Buys: the larger site's works, fleet toward 25–30 machines, and the two deferred technology layers at pilot scale: an **RCW Node pilot** (a dozen Heavy-Nodes at the <$15 unit target, Volume 13 and the digest's BOM) on the excavator fleet, and a **simple booking app** — off-the-shelf scheduling SaaS ($30–80/month) first, RC WORLD OS development only when Volume 13's roadmap says the frequency data justifies it.
- **Effect:** the business crosses from founder-plus-helper to a small operation with rostered staff, and — critically for Rung 5 — starts producing the *instrumented* utilization data (Node telemetry, per-machine hours, booking-system cohorts) that investor diligence will actually read.

### 9.6 Rung 5 — The raise

- **Trigger:** the mini-park hitting its own utilization gates for two consecutive quarters; a clean 24-month trading history; the founder's honest judgment that the catchment supports Volume 2's model.
- **Action:** the external raise — **$2.6 M per Volume 10, Chapter 3's tranche structure — using Volumes 1–13 as the prospectus, unmodified**, with this volume's trading history appended as the demand evidence the original plan could only project. The Micro-Park/mini-park does not close: it becomes the proof-of-demand exhibit, the staff-training site, and eventually the first candidate for Volume 12's franchise playbook.
- **Effect:** Phase 1 begins, and this volume's job is done.

## 10. Risks & Reality Check

### 10.1 The register

| # | Risk | Likelihood | Impact | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Demand risk** — the catchment doesn't care | Medium | Fatal | Demo events *before* lease signature (§8.3) as the cheapest demand probe; 6-month lease with break clause; stop-loss criteria below |
| 2 | **Seasonality** — school terms, summer holidays away, winter (outdoor variant) | High | Medium | Indoor site preference (§3.3); party/school channels fill troughs; reserve sized at 3 months' fixed costs (§6.5) |
| 3 | **Founder burnout** — every weekend, every repair, one person | High | High | Wave format caps intensity; Monday dark day, non-negotiable; helper hired at 50% weekend fill; Rung 3 explicitly buys relief; the §6.5 draw means the founder is *paid*, which matters more to durability than founders admit |
| 4 | **Machine downtime with no backup fleet** | Medium | Medium | 1592 hot-spare; spares tub + weekly PM (Ch. 7); wave roster degrades gracefully — 7 seats sell almost as well as 8; worst realistic case (a 1580 dead awaiting parts) costs ~25% of capacity, not closure |
| 5 | **Landlord/lease risk** — sale, re-let, use-class complaint | Low–Med | High | Checklist §3.5 front-loads the hard questions; make-good kept trivial (membrane, bolt-down-nothing); layout deliberately relocatable in two van-days |
| 6 | **Insurance/liability at micro scale** | Low | Fatal if uncovered | Public liability from day one (E1 — never cut); waiver + verbal Toolbox Talk every wave; the physical rule that customers never enter the machine zone is the real control — geometry beats paperwork |
| 7 | **Theft/vandalism** | Low–Med | Medium | Indoor bay + alarm preferred; transmitters and packs home or in lockbox nightly; fleet is serialized and photographed; machines are conspicuous and hard to fence |
| 8 | **LiPo fire** | Low | Fatal | The §7.5 absolutes; Bat-Safe box; presence rule; storage charge; retirement discipline. This is the risk that gets zero budget mercy |
| 9 | **Price-tier misjudgment** | Medium | Medium | Tier chosen from cinema-ticket benchmark + demo-event willingness-to-pay; held 6 months then reviewed with roster data |
| 10 | **Import failure** — DOA machines, customs seizure, 8-week freight | Medium | Medium | Contingency §2.3 items (1)–(2); retail fallback pricing already in the fleet table; never order the whole fleet from one seller |

### 10.2 The stop-loss

Defined now, while it is theoretical. **Review gate: end of Month 6.** The market is judged absent — not resting, absent — if *all three* hold despite full execution of the Chapter 8 playbook: (a) trailing-3-month net below $250/month; (b) weekend slot fill below 20%; (c) repeat rate below 10% on punch-card evidence. If the gate fails, the decision is wind-down within 60 days — not "one more season," which is how $10K experiments become $25K ones. If exactly one criterion fails, one 90-day remediation cycle is permitted (usually a price-tier or site change), then the gate re-runs, once.

**True capital at risk ≈ $7,000–7,300** — plus six months of the founder's time, which is real and belongs in the decision. Against that: the downside of the master plan's Phase 1 misjudging the same demand question is measured in millions. This volume is, among everything else, the cheapest insurance policy Volumes 1–13 could buy.
