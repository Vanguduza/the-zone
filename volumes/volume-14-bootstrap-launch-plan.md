## 1. The Micro-Park Thesis

### 1.1 The whole park in miniature

Revision 1.0 of this volume argued a one-product-deep launch: construction only, everything else deferred. The founder has overruled it, deliberately and with open eyes, and the revised thesis is this: **RC WORLD's product is not an excavator ride — it is a multi-zone miniature industrial world, and a launch that does not demonstrate the world does not test the business.** The Micro-Park therefore stages every division of the master plan at the smallest scale at which each is honestly itself:

- **Construction & Mining — the anchor zone.** The signature dig-and-haul experience on all-metal, electric, electromechanical (lead-screw) Huina machines. **No hydraulics anywhere in the bootstrap fleet** — the first hydraulic machine (Kabolite class) is explicitly deferred to Rung 2 of the upgrade ladder, exactly per the two-tier doctrine of Volume 4 §1.3.
- **Motorsport — 1/10 scale, per the founder's explicit instruction.** A compact technical circuit on the bare concrete floor: Volume 3's Track A surface doctrine at micro scale, run in drift/gymkhana format with speed managed by layout tightness and throttle-limited transmitters. The fleet is built on the founder's original racing doctrine (source PDF; Volume 3 chapter 1 doctrine block): one budget 1/10 chassis bought in multiples, common electronics and batteries, a different body shell on each car for identity, and each car tuned to a distinct handling personality while lap-time parity stays within 2–3% — winners decided by driver skill, never by the car.
- **Crawler course.** A technical timber-and-rock strip for MN/WPL-class crawlers — Track C's DNA in 24 m².
- **Aviation — the Whoop Arcade as the entire division.** A fully netted micro-drone cage flying 1S Tiny-Whoop-class quads. Volume 5 §6.4 built the Whoop Arcade as a side attraction; at micro scale it *is* the Aviation Division. No fixed-wing, no helicopters, no EDF — those need the 60 × 40 m cage that waits at Rung 4+.
- **Marine.** An above-ground frame-pool basin with mini electric boats, run under Volume 6's harbour-games logic and scale-speed discipline (§2.4).
- **Agriculture.** A tractor-and-implement demo cell inside the construction zone, per the master plan's own structure (Agriculture lives within the Construction Division; Volume 4 Chapter 7).

### 1.2 The honest trade-off: breadth versus depth

The trade-off must be stated plainly, because the founder is choosing its costs on purpose. **One product deep** concentrates $10,000 into one excellent zone: more machines per zone, more redundancy, one maintenance pattern, one marketing message, and a simpler solo operation. **Every division shallow** spreads the same money across five small fleets: each zone has minimal redundancy (one front-line excavator; four cars; three whoops), the founder maintains five different failure ecosystems, and no single zone reaches the density that makes it spectacular on its own.

The founder chose breadth anyway, for three reasons this volume accepts as governing. **First, the demand experiment is the right one.** The master plan's bet is a *park*, not an attraction; only a multi-zone launch measures cross-zone behaviour — which zones pull visits, which retain, what a wristband is worth versus a single ticket (Chapter 9 is designed to harvest exactly this data). **Second, breadth is the differentiator at micro scale too.** A 300 m² dig floor is a curiosity; a 300 m² *world* — dig, race, crawl, fly, sail — is a destination and a birthday-party product no competitor in Volume 2's landscape can match. **Third, the Micro-Park becomes a working scale model of the prospectus.** When Rung 5's investor asks what RC WORLD is, the answer is not a deck — it is a visit. Each micro division proves a specific master-plan claim: construction proves the simulation-of-work thesis (Volume 4 Chapter 1), motorsport proves parity-fleet racing sells (Volume 3), the whoop cage proves the FPV on-ramp works with the general public (Volume 5 §6.4), marine proves water calms and broadens the demographic mix (Volume 6 §1.2), and the crawler strip proves the patience products retain.

The risk this decision buys — five thin fleets, one founder, spread attention — is carried openly on the Chapter 11 risk register as the **spread-thin risk**, with its mitigations. It is not hidden, and Rung 1 of the upgrade ladder exists precisely to correct it *with demand data instead of guesses*: the first reinvestment deepens whichever zone the roster proves busiest.

### 1.3 What is retained, what is deferred

Retained at micro scale, from the canon: the **20-minute Shift** as the billing unit with the ~30% battery buffer returned; the **1:3 excavator:dump-truck ratio**; the **LiPo 3.4–4.2 V/cell window**, **3:1 battery:vehicle ratio** and **XT60 standard**; customers **never enter live zones**; daily inspections and spares-with-fleet ordering; racing parity within **2–3%**. Deferred, with restoration points on the ladder: RCW Node telemetry and RC WORLD OS (manual roster + phone timers until Rung 3–4), Wi-Fi mesh, hydraulics (Rung 2), full-format division geometries and all buildings (Rung 4), staff beyond a weekend helper (Rung 3).

> **Investor Note.** Nothing in this volume revises the master plan's economics. The Micro-Park is a demand experiment and an operator apprenticeship run at the bottom of the pricing ladder. What transfers to the Rung 5 prospectus is the demand signal per zone, the retention data, the cross-zone behaviour statistics — and a founder who has personally rebuilt every gearbox, ESC and stuffing box in a five-division fleet.

### 1.4 Success criteria

Set now, so they cannot be moved later: **(a)** 300+ paid Shift-equivalents/month by Month 6; **(b)** ≥25% of weekend customers returning within 90 days (punch-card evidence — the micro echo of Volume 10 §10.4's second-visit KPI); **(c)** ≥3 party bookings/month by Month 6; **(d)** monthly net inside the Chapter 9 corridor; **(e)** — new to Revision 2.0 — a clean **zone-demand ranking**: by Month 6 the roster must show which two zones carry the park, because Rung 1 spends against that ranking. Miss the Chapter 11 stop-loss instead, and the fleet sells at the Chapter 11 recovery rates having cost ≈ $7,000 to test what Phase 1 would have tested for $2.6 M.

### 2.3 Adjacency logic

The plan is not decorative; every placement answers a separation or supervision problem:

- **Dust.** The dig pit sits at the far east wall, 12+ m from the racing surface. Sand is kept at 4–6% moisture by mister (Volume 4 §6.6 scaled), the pit is membrane-lined and timber-edged, and the spine physically interrupts tracked-sand migration. Grit on a drift line changes lap times — the parity audit (Chapter 5) assumes a swept surface, so the track is at the opposite end from the sand.
- **Water.** The marine basin is 21 m from the charging corner — water and charging never share a neighbourhood. The basin cell keeps a 1.0–1.5 m splash margin on membrane; the pool is covered outside marine blocks. The nearest dig face is across the spine and 4+ m away; sand stays misted, water stays covered, and the two coexist.
- **Noise.** Whoops whine, excavators hum, race tires scrub. The whoop cage is netted (which also baffles), and the party nook sits beside the entry — the two social functions cluster away from the pilot bench, where FPV concentration needs relative calm.
- **Single-operator sight lines.** The spine is the design's core: one straight 25 m line from which **every zone is fully visible**. The founder standing at mid-spine (the crawler/marine junction) sees both live zones of any Chapter 8 session block. All customer operation happens *from* the spine rail — excavator stations, race driver boxes, crawler line, pilot bench, marine helm — so the canon "customers never enter live zones" is enforced by geometry, not vigilance. Staff gates (`#`) are the only way in.
- **Entry theatre.** The pay desk faces the construction anchor: the first thing a visitor sees is a metal excavator swinging a loaded bucket. The party nook's tables overlook marine and whoop — the calmest zones to linger beside with cake.

### 2.4 Site route — options analysis

| Factor | A. Indoor warehouse bay | B. Outdoor fenced yard | C. Partnership / pop-up inside existing venue | D. Mobile trailer rig |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Weather | Immune; every booked block happens | Rain/heat kill days; 30–50% seasonal swing | Immune (host roof) | Weather-exposed at most stops |
| Fit to divisions | **Drift needs smooth concrete; whoops need still air; pool needs security — all native indoors** | Drift surface must be built; wind ruins whoop flying outdoors at 1S weight | Depends entirely on host floor | Cannot carry the pool or pit credibly; park shrinks to demos |
| Cost | Deposit + $400–800/mo | Lower rent; lighting/power often absent | Lowest cash; revenue share instead | $4–7K trailer consumes the budget |
| Control & hours | Full control; evening sessions year-round | Daylight-bound without lighting spend | Host's hours, host's rules, host's veto | Total flexibility, zero permanence |
| Security | Roller door + alarm; fleet sleeps on site | Theft exposure; nightly teardown or lockbox | Host security | Vehicle theft risk concentrates everything |
| Brand signal | A real place — bookable, reviewable, Google-mappable | Real but seasonal | Borrowed identity | Event vendor, not a park |

**Disqualifiers surfaced by the analysis:** the whoop cage and the drift circuit — two of the five divisions — are effectively indoor-only products at this scale (1S whoops are helpless in wind; gymkhana grip consistency dies on rough or dusty outdoor slab). A mobile rig cannot stage the pit or the pool, which guts the whole-park thesis.

**Recommended route: A — indoor warehouse bay**, month-to-month or 6-month lease with a break clause. Option C is the named fallback *if* a family-entertainment-centre or farm-market host offers ≥250 m² of smooth indoor slab with acceptable hours — footfall is worth a revenue share when the floor is right. B and D are rejected for this scope (B re-enters at Rung 4 where full-format outdoor zones return; D survives only as the Chapter 10 demo-day kit).

### 2.5 Landlord checklist

Walk in with the one-page concept sheet (a photo of a Huina 1580 digging, the layout above, the insurance quote) and resolve every line before a handshake:

- [ ] **Sand on the floor** acceptable over membrane, with a make-good clause (sweep-out + membrane removal) at exit?
- [ ] **Water on the floor**: a 3.05 m pool at 500 mm working depth holds ≈ 3.7 t over 7.3 m² ≈ **500 kg/m²** — confirm slab rating and any objection to an indoor basin; agree the fill (≈ 3,700 L, hose from a tap) and the drain path (submersible pump to a gully or yard)
- [ ] **Floor loading** overall: ~8 t of sand over 20 m² of pit/loop ≈ 0.4 t/m² — trivial, but ask before they do
- [ ] **Public access** permitted under the use class/zoning? Children on premises?
- [ ] **Operating hours** — evenings and weekends are the business
- [ ] Any objection to **LiPo charging** on site? (Show the Bat-Safe box and the Chapter 11 fire regime — lead with the answer)
- [ ] **Power**: circuits in the bay, breaker ratings, RCD present, meter responsibility (needs: one dedicated charging circuit, one general)
- [ ] **Water/toilet** access — a tap serves the pool and the sand mister; a toilet within 50 m is required in practice for the party channel
- [ ] **Insurance**: landlord named on the policy? Minimum cover figure?
- [ ] **Lease**: month-to-month or 6-month with a 2-month break — long enough to test, short enough to stop-loss
- [ ] **Signage** rights at the road/door; launch-weekend banner permitted?
- [ ] **Noise** constraints (machines are quiet; birthday parties are not), and who else uses the building on weekends — forklift traffic, shared doors, alarm schedules

> **Trade Hack.** Offer the landlord a family Grand Tour voucher with the first rent payment. A landlord whose grandchildren have dug, drifted, crawled, flown and sailed in one visit renews leases, forgives sand in the corridor, and becomes the building's word-of-mouth node. Cost: ~$30 of Shift value. Return: a relationship the stop-loss clause hopes never to test.

### 5.2 The personality fleet — same hardware, different soul

| # | Name | Shell | ESC / throttle | Suspension | Ballast | Character |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **The Slider** | Drift wagon | 50% punch, 55% top throttle | Soft rear, firm front | None | Loose rear — rewards smooth inputs; punishes ham fists |

> **Field Note.** Customers choose a **name**, not a model number. "I want The Snap" is the product. The shell colour and the card on the pit wall sell the fantasy; the parity work sells the fairness.

### 5.3 Track layout — 80 m², ~40 m line

On the bare concrete 10 × 8 m cell:

- **Surface:** swept and damp-mopped before racing blocks — dust is the parity enemy.
- **Layout:** figure-eight with a tight hairpin and a sweeper; total line ~38–42 m. Cones mark apexes; PVC pool-noodle kerbs on the hairpin only.
- **Speed control:** layout tightness + throttle caps above — target **walking pace to light jog** for adults; no straight longer than 6 m.
- **Barriers:** options analysis:

| Barrier | Cost | Pros | Cons | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| PVC pipe + joints | ~$90 | Reconfigurable; light | Tips if hit hard | **Recommended** |
| Timber sleepers | ~$140 | Solid | Damage to cars and floor | No |
| Tire walls | ~$0 (scavenge) | Realistic | Dirty, heavy, trip hazard | No at indoor micro |

### 5.4 Race formats

- **Gymkhana time trial:** phone stopwatch, three laps, best two count — leaderboard on a whiteboard.
- **Head-to-head:** two cars, staggered start, same layout — marshal calls penalties (cone hits = +2 s).
- **Energy-management game (Volume 3 §11.2 scaled):** each driver gets **12 throttle-"boosts"** (honour system or clicker); smooth drivers finish faster because they never hit the cap. Phone timer only — RCW Node deferred to Rung 4.

## 8. Solo Operations Across Five Zones

### 8.1 The operational problem

One founder cannot run five live zones simultaneously. **Something must rotate.**

| Model | How it works | Pros | Cons | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| A. All zones open, customers self-serve | Chaos | None | Unsafe; batteries die unnoticed | **Reject** |
| B. Appointment-only one zone at a time | Simple | Safe | Kills "whole park" birthday promise | Reject |
| **C. Three-block session rotation** | Zones grouped; all customers rotate together | One operator; predictable; whole park tasted per visit | No zone open "on demand" off-schedule | **Recommended** |
| D. Two zones + "bonus queue" | Flexible | Better utilisation | Complex to explain | Month 6+ if demand data supports |

**Recommended route: C — three-block Grand Tour** (75 minutes customer time, three × 20-min Shifts + 5-min transitions):

| Block | Zones live | Customer activity |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Construction + Agriculture | Excavator or truck Shift |
| 2 | Motorsport + Crawler | Race Shift OR crawl Shift (customer chooses at block start) |
| 3 | Whoop + Marine | Whoop Shift + boat Shift (halves of block if busy) |

Between blocks: **battery swap + quick sweep** — 5 minutes, narrated as "pit lane changeover" (immersion).

### 8.2 Capacity mathematics

| Zone | Seats / block | Blocks/day (weekend) | Max Shifts/day |
|---|---|---|---|
| Construction | 4 machines | 6 | 24 |
| Motorsport | 4 cars | 6 | 24 |
| Crawler | 3 | 6 | 18 |
| Whoop | 2 | 6 | 12 |
| Marine | 2 boats | 6 | 12 |
| **Grand Tour slots** | **8 customers / wave** | **6 waves** | **48 seat-blocks** |

At **$8 Grand Tour** (Chapter 9): 48 × $8 = **$384/day** weekend ceiling from tours alone; parties and walk-in single-zone Shifts add on top.

### 8.3 Battery rotation SOP (one person)

1. End of block → collect all packs from returning machines (customers leave vehicles at rail).
2. Voltage check — any cell <3.5 V → charge lane; ≥3.7 V → rest bin.
3. Install rested packs ≥3.9 V for next block.
4. Charging only in Bat-Safe during operating hours; never leave charging unattended overnight in the box on site.

### 8.4 Daily open / close checklists

**Open**

- [ ] Sweep race floor; mist sand; cover pool if not marine block first
- [ ] Voltage-check entire battery pool; top up rest bin
- [ ] Test one machine per zone (30 s each)
- [ ] Cash/float; waivers; whiteboard leaderboard reset
- [ ] Net door latched; barriers aligned

**Close**

- [ ] All LiPo at storage voltage (3.80–3.85 V/cell) or charging supervised to storage
- [ ] Sand covered; pool drained or covered
- [ ] Fleet wiped; damage log updated
- [ ] Cash reconciled; tomorrow's roster noted

### 8.5 Toolbox Talk (simplified)

Verbal + signed paper waiver until RC WORLD OS exists: battery rules (never enter zones; stop when buzzer sounds); whoop goggles hygiene; pool edge rule (no running); parent's responsibility for under-12s. **90 seconds**, every first-time visitor.

### 12.5 Cost-effectiveness analysis

**Break-even vs buying:**

- Average AliExpress structure: **$35** shipped; wait 3 weeks; not quite your layout.
- Average printed structure: **~$4 filament** + 8 h print + 2 h design = **founder time is the real cost**.
- **Printer pays for itself after ~10–12 custom pieces** ($370 ÷ $35 ≈ 11).

At bootstrap, founder time is cheaper than cash — but **only after the park is open**. Hence: printer at **Rung 1**, not Day 1.

**Working conveyor (optional upgrade):**

> **Safety Warning.** PETG only within 2 m of the sand pit (humidity). PLA creeps in heat — do not use for load-bearing conveyor frames. **Never** print LiPo holders from PLA — use metal or commercial bags only.
